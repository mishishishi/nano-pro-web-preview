SolutionsExterior surfaces

Surfaces thatclean themselves

One mineral, solvent-free coating for the outside of a building: facades, concrete, paving, wood, statues and heritage stone. It is vapour-permeable, and daylight is what activates it.

Conventional protection resists. This layer reacts.

What the weatherdoes to a surface

  • Soiling and growth

    Surfaces darken, and algae, lichens, moss and mould take hold wherever they stay damp.

  • Degradation

    Ultraviolet light breaks down the binder, and water, oxygen, CO₂, de-icing salts and temperature cycling erode concrete.

  • Passive treatment

    Silicone paints and impregnations resist for a while, then need cleaning and chemical treatment again.

A passive coating does not end the cycle. It sets its interval.

How it workson a facade

The coating is a mineral layer 10–30 µm thick, left open to the light. It decomposes the organic contamination that lands on it, denies microorganisms a foothold, and shields the substrate's own binder from ultraviolet light.

It is vapour-permeable, so the wall behind it still breathes, and it releases graffiti without damaging what is underneath.

Nothing is added after the coat but light.

Where itis used

  • Commercial buildings

    Facades of offices, retail and representative buildings, and the heritage-listed stone that cannot be stripped and repainted.

  • Residential

    Houses and terraces, fences, and the wooden structures around them.

  • Infrastructure

    Concrete structures, paving, statues and monuments — the public surfaces that are hardest to take out of service.

Different buildings, one exposure problem: weather, light, and whatever grows in between.

What itchanges

  • Self-cleaning

    Daylight decomposes the organic contamination that lands on the surface, so it stays clean without being washed.

  • UV protection

    The layer takes the ultraviolet light that would otherwise break down the substrate's own binder.

  • Anti-graffiti

    Graffiti comes off in 10 minutes with a brush and pressure water. The durable anti-graffiti build needs 6+ coats and white surfaces only.

  • It cleans the air, too

    FN NANO® calculates that 1 m² of treated surface purifies around 3 million m³ of air a year, at an average airflow of 2 m/s and 8 hours of daylight.

What itadds up to

Facades still clean after 7 years and more, and wood with no algae after 12 years.

These are FN NANO®'s own worked examples — a dock still clear after 5 years, a building in Šumburk after 6 — and it warrants 10 years of full functionality on a coated facade. What third parties measured, including NOx degradation at the Czech Academy of Sciences and Universita di Torino, is in the document library.
